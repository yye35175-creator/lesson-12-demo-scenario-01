# Approved final exam marking criteria

### Category scoring

| Category | Weight | Deterministic Evidence |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Group runnable SOA application package | 30% | File structure, startup, API endpoints, database schema and seed data, frontend-backend integration, validation, error handling, and project organisation. |
| Container and CI/CD evidence | 20% | Dockerfile, docker-compose.yml, reproducible startup, health checks, test commands, CI/CD workflow files, logs, and artefacts. |
| Group automated test suite and debugging evidence | 20% | Student tests, hidden marker tests, response-code checks, failure-case checks, debug log, and submitted runtime logs. |
| Structured technical explanation | 15% | Machine-marked group-exam-answer.json and individual reflection checks covering architecture, API design, database design, retrieval quality, technology trade-offs, source evaluation, ethics, and AI-use declaration. |
| Individual contribution, peer review, and professional evidence | 15% | Git history or equivalent contribution evidence, branch or pull-request evidence where available, issue/task records, timestamped contribution log, Groupwork Peer Review evidence, source acknowledgements, integrity declaration, and punctual submission. |

| **Container and CI/CD evidence** | 20% | The submitted container and CI/CD evidence is complete, repeatable, and professionally organised, passing automated checks for Dockerfile quality, compose configuration, reproducible startup, health checks, test commands, CI logs, artefacts, and deployment-readiness evidence. | The submitted container and CI/CD configuration is repeatable and passes most automated checks for Dockerfile, compose services, startup behaviour, health checks, test execution, and CI logs or artefacts, with minor configuration or documentation gaps. | Container and CI/CD evidence supports the main application workflow, including basic Docker or compose configuration, startup commands, test commands, and submitted logs or artefacts, with some gaps in repeatability, health checks, environment handling, or explanation. | Docker or CI/CD files are present but incomplete, non-repeatable, disconnected from the application workflow, or unable to build, start, test, or produce usable logs through the automated marker. | No usable Docker, service configuration, startup evidence, health check, CI/CD workflow, or automation artefact is submitted. |

**GROUP PEER REVIEW RUBRIC**

Where a groupwork assignment is part of a course’s assessment, a peer review procedure must take place so that all final scores are a fair judgment of the work undertaken by each individual student.

On this form you must record your grade for the amount and value of contribution that you feel that each member of your group provided.

You must be fair and justify the score you are giving in this peer review, based on the ratings below:

| Criteria: | Excellent | Very Good | Satisfactory | Poor | Fail |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Research Effort and Quality** | The individual provided an excellent level of effort and quality. | The individual provided a high level of effort and quality. | The individual provided a satisfactory level of effort and quality.  | The individual barely engaged, showing minimal effort and poor quality research. | The individual did not engage, and  provided no research and no effort to support this activity. |
| **Teamwork** | Engaged in all in-person or digital discussions, and never missed a team meeting (unless with good reason, such as illness).  Provided a very important role in the group. | Engaged in most in-person or digital discussions, and never missed a team meeting (unless with good reason, such as illness). Provided an important role in the group. | Occasionally engaged in in-person or digital discussions, and missed some team meetings, without a good reason. Was of some assistance in supporting the team. | Rarely engaged in in-person or digital discussions, and frequently missed team meetings, without a good reason.  Was of very limited assistance in the team. | Did not engage in in-person or digital discussions, and missed most team meetings, without a good reason.  Largely failed to address the remit set and was a significant hindrance to the team. |
| **Professionalism** | Excellent professional response to the required work, and acted respectfully in always meeting the requirements well ahead of timeline.. | Very good professional response to the required work, and acted respectfully in delivering to agreed timeline. | Satisfactory professional response to the required work, but sometimes needed to be reminded to deliver to agreed timeline. | Poor professional response to the required work, and rarely communicated on work delivery and/or did not deliver to deadlines, which negatively impacted other team members.    | Did not respond professionally to the required work, and did not communicate or deliver to deadline and/or promised to deliver and did not deliver to deadlines, which severely negatively impacted other team members.    |
| **Improvement needed?** | Very little scope for improvement. | Some minor areas for improvement. | Some major areas improvement. | Considerable improvement required. | Fundamental improvement required. |

To receive the full Groupwork course grade, you must receive 80% (on average) from your peer review within your Group. This means you would have been scored with very good to excellent standards in this rubric).

Any student that feels they may have been scored unfairly in their peer review must discuss with their Course Leader, so that this can be reviewed.
